# Offline measured terrain source

The snow-mountain production renderer can replace its authored hills with a verified 5 × 5 km crop near the Everest South Col. This is an offline-only geometry source; it is not loaded by the website and is not navigation data. The local walking platform remains authored rather than a surveyed reconstruction; surrounding near and far terrain evaluate one continuous function combining the measured height, platform blending and explicitly authored relief. The current r10 source includes a DEM-limited art-directed summit drop; it must not be presented as an unmodified survey.

## Acquisition and integration

```powershell
node scripts/fetch-terrain-source.cjs
python scripts/refine-terrain.py
```

The downloader stores only under `.render-work/terrain/`, verifies compressed and decompressed hashes before replacement, refuses unexpected revisions, and records a local manifest. The raw DEM must remain out of Git.

Pass `--measured-terrain` to `scripts/render-world-panorama.py` to enable the verified terrain hook. It loads `scripts/refine-terrain.py` after importing the base scene and before adding continuous foreground snow detail. Only a refined `snowmountain` render is eligible. The hook requires one exact 48,841-vertex, 1,500 m descriptor/mesh match and one exact 40,401-vertex near-patch match before refinement. It validates every imported vertex against the descriptor, requires an identity far-terrain transform, and checks both the walking region and near/far seam to below 0.1 mm before hiding the original far terrain. Matching or numerical verification failures leave the source meshes unchanged. The near mesh is copied before refinement; scene descriptors and browser exploration geometry are not modified.

The chosen anchor is 27.9718 N, 86.9300 E, bilinear DEM height 7,908.16 m. Blender X is east, Blender Y is north, Z is height minus the anchor. Horizontal and vertical DEM scales are both 1:1. The platform remains at zero height over east ±14 m and north −21 to +6 m, including a 2 m margin around the actual walking bounds (east ±12 m, north −19 to +4 m). Outside that rectangle, the DEM contribution is multiplied by quintic smootherstep of the Euclidean distance to the platform divided by 75 m. Reaching 75 m means only that this blend has reached full weight; the final surface is not wholly measured terrain there.

In the r10 source, `_summit_apron` retains the DEM-limited negative authored offset introduced in r9 outside the protected base platform. It descends over the first 24 m, begins recovering at 34 m and reaches exactly zero at 110 m distance from the rectangle. Its depth is limited by the real surrounding DEM: samples are taken 55, 110 and 175 m farther outward along the radial direction, and the shallowest available descent multiplied by 0.32 limits the deformation, with 86 m retained only as an absolute upper bound. If any sampled terrain is at or above the anchor, this permitted depth is zero. The effect is strongest to the west, with north and south directional weights of 0.83 and 0.46; local fracture modulation further reduces it. The eastern route retains the DEM-driven rise. The earlier unrendered 86 m/242 m proposal and its 85.09 m sample are superseded: they could create an artificial deep trough before recovering into a shallower DEM. This revised deformation is still deliberate summit composition, not measured topography, additional survey precision or a reconstruction of an actual Everest route.

Beyond 80 m radius from the origin, slope-weighted artistic rock relief fades in over 160 m. The current source combines a 65 m noise term with coefficient 7 m, a warped directional joint/ridge term with coefficient 16 m, and a smaller directional noise term with coefficient 3.4 m that fades out by 1,700 m. The previous 10.8 m combined bound no longer describes this function. These additions still apply where the apron has vanished; neither 75 m nor 110 m marks a boundary beyond which the final mesh is purely measured data.

Both near and far patches evaluate that same height function. Their 34.090909 m half-extent square boundary is checked at all 800 original perimeter samples; positions, world-scale UVs, shared finite-difference height-gradient normals and slope-driven snow coverage match there. The r10 clear and dusk Blender build logs both report a 0.000001814947 m seam error, zero walking-base error and the same 236,800 far vertices and 471,200 faces. Local descriptor verification covers all 4,757 walking-region vertices. The earlier final-r9 full-mesh numerical check verified finite positions and snow coverage in [0, 1]; its unchanged geometry has first-ring heights of -20.5598 to +1.6529 m and sampled authored descent of -15.1618 to 0 m, not the 86 m absolute bound. The r10 build log does not record snow-coverage extrema. Its revised coverage function is explicitly clamped; the previous local coverage extrema must not be represented as newly logged r10 measurements. Selected north and west sections descend into adjacent terrain without the earlier deep recovery trough. These checks do not establish visual quality or survey accuracy.

Independent decorative wind-sculpted snow relief is applied afterward: 235 irregular, asymmetric elongated formations and three broader wind lips, protected camera/prop footprints, and zero displacement at the seam. The actual r10 clear and dusk builds each contain 1,442,401 near-surface vertices with 5.6818 cm nominal spacing, relief ranging from −0.0437278806 to +0.3614772494 m, 1,347,957 displaced vertices, and a minimum steep-side Gaussian e-fold width of 19.63 cm. All 800 original boundary vertices remain unchanged. These full-build values supersede preliminary local displacement samples; they describe authored detail, not new measured resolution. The rejected r9 experiment zeroed decorative displacement across the entire expanded platform, leaving a visibly flat foreground. R10 removes that full-platform mask and its 2.8 m fade, restoring the r8 wind-ridge treatment while preserving the unchanged walking base and exact camera/prop contacts. The earlier r7 lee-side cross-section was narrower than its mesh spacing and produced repeated saw-tooth bands; r8 increased sampling and smoothed that physical cross-section instead of hiding the artifact in a texture. These offline snow details do not change browser walking collisions. The later landscape pass skips its legacy snow geometry and material adjustments when the foreground uses a `verified_dem_snow_rock` material, preserving the protected camera, props and boundary. The far perimeter extends to 2,500 m in each direction with a downward edge skirt.

Near and far terrain share a material tagged `verified_dem_snow_rock`. Slope, wind exposure, concavity and low-frequency breakup determine snow deposition versus exposed rock; the later foreground-detail pass must not wash this material back to uniform white. The material uses all three verified seamless `rock_face_03` channels and macro-scale color variation. Near rock diffuse, roughness and normals share three world-position projections at an authored 2.7 m repeat, with shared normal-dependent blend weights. Each projected tangent-space normal is converted to world space before its deviation is added to the geometric surface normal. This replaces the rejected intermediate mix of box-projected color and unrelated UV-mapped normals. R9 still produced visible distant grid repetition in color and roughness despite its normal filtering. R10 fades those scanned color and roughness channels over 45–210 m into low-frequency slate-gray albedo and roughness 0.88; near scanned color saturation is reduced to 0.12. Rock normal strength fades from 0.35 to zero over 30–220 m, not the earlier 0.015 distant floor. Thus fine details remain at close range without extending an obvious repeating texture grid over remote slopes. R10 also restores the r8 slope-deposition thresholds, reduces the concavity contribution to −0.08/+0.12 and the procedural coverage breakup to ±0.14, avoiding the r9 broad brown exposed-rock patch on a relatively gentle slope. Snow uses the verified `snow_01` roughness channel and a subdued normal contribution, plus explicit world-position wind-scoured and sub-millimetre grain detail. Its diffuse map is intentionally not tiled because r4 visual inspection found visibly repeating footprints. The r5 experiment with constant-height wave bands was removed after actual renders showed artificial contour stripes; world-position normals alone also proved insufficiently visible without medium-scale snow geometry. The model-atlas `rock_face_01` is not tiled onto the landscape. Textured normal maps and shader relief are artistic surface detail, not added survey resolution.

Clear and dusk r8 renders have been independently inspected in all six view directions and installed as review resources. The earlier vertical walls, regular contour stripes and r7 ridge aliasing are gone. Ten individually reshaped, partly buried outcrops replace the repeated set of 21; seven oval snow pads are removed. An original soft expedition pack, surface-fitted straps and hardware, grounded rope and pole replace the box-like proxy. Foreground snow remains too smooth, distant rock/snow boundaries lack convincing natural detail, and the platform composition remains flat. These renders do not pass the photorealism target. Further changes require another complete visual review; no photo-real or survey-accuracy certification follows from the data checks. Keep the measured terrain source separate from the independently authored snow surface details.

The actual r9 clear image was rejected rather than installed: its zero-displacement foreground visibly regressed to a flat gray-white slab, while a distant brown patch exposed a repeating rock-texture grid. Local numerical success did not override that visual failure.

Clear and dusk r10 images have now been independently inspected in all six directions, with enlarged rectilinear checks of the rear snow ridges and right-hand exposed-rock patch. Both are approved to replace r8 only as review baselines. Foreground wind relief is restored without r7 saw-tooth artifacts; the brown repeating grid is gone, leaving a smaller cool-gray patch. No new visible near/far seam or floating equipment was observed. The actual build reports ten retained individual outcrops, seven removed oval snow pads, five replaced pack-proxy pieces and 54 new equipment objects. `refine-snow-props.py` replaces 22 uniformly repeated proxy railing parts with five irregular west-side anchors and sagging fixed-line rope; the anchors remain outside the real east ±12 m walking bounds.

R10 still does not reach the requested photographic result: the observer stands on a broad snow terrace rather than a convincing knife-edge summit; the snow surface remains too soft and clean, distant rock surfaces look sculpted, and rock/snow boundaries retain a creamy or airbrushed appearance. These residual defects remain visible in both lighting phases. Two inspected lighting variants are not two clean acceptance rounds. Photorealistic approval remains zero clean rounds.
